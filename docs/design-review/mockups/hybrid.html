  1. Peer-reviewed · presenting at ASSETS 2026

    Ableism Unchecked: A Literature Review of Disability Misconceptions in Computing Education

    Yasmine N. Elglaly, Ryan Critchlow, Katherine Baker, and Kristen Shinohara

    ASSETS 2026, Porto, Portugal. Talk: [session, day and time]

    Accessibility coursework can produce measurable knowledge gains while students' reflections still contain ableist framings: deficit-based language, unsupported claims about assistive technology, and erasure of disabled people's agency.

    My contribution: I applied an iteratively developed codebook to all 35 papers in the review corpus and helped identify six recurring patterns of ableism. A second researcher reviewed the findings for consistency. This is my first conference presentation.

About

I'm interested in the work that happens before the code: figuring out what people need, deciding what gets built, and keeping the thing that ships close to the problem it started with. I've done versions of that as a support analyst, a graduate researcher, and now in a wood shop.

Most of what I know about building for people I learned in the KIND Lab. I scrapped my own approved study design because I had planned it for autistic kids without involving any of them. The replacement put me in four families' living rooms watching them play.

Off the clock: trails with Georgia, half marathons, road biking, and making cocktails.
